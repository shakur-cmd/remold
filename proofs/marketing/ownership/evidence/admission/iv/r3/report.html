Your calls

  • Close the slice now, or after two small fixes? I recommend after. Suppression held in every case I tried: no DNC was ever lost. But the reconciler can still change an existing owner's email, and a single long first name stops a whole run. Both fixes are about one line each (see D-R3-1 and D-R3-2), and a re-check would take about 20 minutes. If you close now, write down that the public write path is closed at SERVICE level and the reconciler is open with these two defects.
  • Abuse (N-1) needs a policy before any live form. One edge took 113 posts per second with no limit. The reconciler turns each fresh email into a real Mautic contact at about 1.9 s each, roughly 1,850 an hour per tenant. So an attacker fills Mautic with junk contacts and the backlog grows about 200 times faster than it drains. I recommend four things. First, rate-limit per client and per email at the edge. Second, keep one pending capture per email per tenant in Convex. Third, cap pending captures per tenant and alert past it. Fourth, create a Mautic contact only after confirmation (double opt-in) or a Remold-side review, not straight from an anonymous form. A signed nonce does not help much here, and I agree with the builder on that.

The public write path, yes. Across three rounds, nothing a public client sends reaches Mautic's form path. Form submissions never moved, and identity and suppression were never changed by a post, with the D1 edit live, under 32-way concurrency and through kills. The reconciler, which feeds public data into Mautic, not yet. Suppression holds everywhere, but D-R3-1 can change an existing contact's email (an identity field), and D-R3-2 can starve it. What I could not check

  • A real, not simulated, persistent Mautic 5xx for a specific capture.
  • Whether Mautic's CSV import stores non-ASCII or padded emails. That decides how reachable D-R3-1's precondition is.
  • Anything on tenant B beyond read-only counts, and the builder's full replay.mjs (it writes to B).
  • The lease under a real skew of the backend clock. The container clock cannot be changed without CAP_SYS_TIME. Expiry is judged only by the backend clock, and I showed that client clock skew is harmless.
