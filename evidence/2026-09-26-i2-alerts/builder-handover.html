How it works now

A cron runs alerts:check every minute. The check does its reading in small queries. Queries never conflict with telemetry:record, which rewrites the metrics rows and scheduled-function rows the old version read inside a mutation.

  1. Scan. It reads _scheduled_functions from a saved watermark, stopping 10 s short of now so it does not read rows still committing. It reads 1000 rows a page, up to 10 pages, within 30 s. Finished runs update a small per-function health row. Unfinished jobs are added to a watch list by job id.
  2. Watch. Jobs 15 or more minutes past their due time are all read. If that cannot finish, the check is behind. Recently due jobs are read only to record their results; leaving some for the next check is ordinary lag. A job still unfinished past the threshold is marked stalled on its watch row. Its alert resolves only when that row goes, which happens when the job finishes, is canceled or disappears.
  3. REST. The check counts caught 500s in the last 5 minutes. The 503 migration pause is excluded. If the window cannot be read, the REST alert is held as it is.
  4. Apply. One mutation writes only alert tables. It compare-and-sets the watermark, so of two overlapping checks only one applies. It opens and resolves alerts and queues one notice per open and per resolve. Anything the check could not read raises the coverage alert.
  5. Send. The check claims one notice, sends it without following redirects, and marks it delivered on a 2xx. After a failure it stops and leaves the rest for the next check. Delivery is at-least-once, so receivers should dedupe by id. Notices are deleted after 7 days.

The schema adds five tables: opsAlerts, opsNotices, opsAlertScan, opsAlertWatch and opsAlertFunctions. No existing table or field changed.

Found on the way

  • Scheduler backlog under REST load. After 5100 REST requests at about 280 per second, the newest 3000 telemetry:record jobs were still pending more than 30 s later. My untested guess is that every request in a minute writes the same metrics row and those mutations retry against each other. It is telemetry behavior, not alert behavior. With the 15-minute default it only alerts if the backlog lasts that long. Worth a look before production traffic.
  • Snapshot drill broken since I1. ops/release/snapshot.mjs and snapshot-drill.mjs did not copy packages/contracts, so no persisted release could get a passing receipt. Before: r2/snapshot-seed-before.txt. After: r2/snapshot-drill.txt.
  • Kept failed runs. Three failed service runs are kept as evidence. service-run-burst-no-catchup.txt: the watch-add cap was too small. service-run-backlog-no-catchup.txt: a real 25000-job backlog does not drain in 10 minutes locally. service-run-watch-lag-as-behind.txt: recently due jobs were wrongly counted as behind, fixed by the two-pass watch.
