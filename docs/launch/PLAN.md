## 1. Decisions for Shakur

1. **Auth and billing decided as a pair: WorkOS AuthKit for identity, Stripe direct for money (recommend).** Remold's orgs, members, and entitlements live in Convex tables by design; direct Stripe keeps billing state in those same tables with Stripe as payment processor and subscription source of truth. Clerk Billing would move plan and organization state into Clerk's models for the same total fee (Stripe Billing pay-as-you-go 0.7% versus Clerk's 0.7% on top of Stripe; both pricing pages fetched 2026-09-24). WorkOS's concrete benefit is the official Convex integration with environment auto-provisioning (docs.convex.dev/auth/authkit), which Step 2's previews lean on; the 1M-free-users headroom is a bonus, not the argument.
2. **Mautic hosting carried from r3 (recommend unchanged):** DigitalOcean 2 GB droplet ~USD 12-14 per month, Amazon SES (~USD 0.16 per 1,000 sends), sending subdomain `mail.codemyvibe.com`. Mautic stays CodeMyVibe-only and operator-configured in v1.
3. **Pricing: one flat workspace plan after a 14-day cardless trial; the monthly number is yours.** Flat per workspace, not per seat; per-seat is a distributed transaction that buys nothing at this scale.
4. **No permanent free tier at launch (recommend: defer).** Trial, then pay or go read-only with export always available. Adding free later is a widening; removing it later is a broken promise.
5. **Convex Professional, USD 25 per month, now (recommend: yes).** It deletes machinery we would otherwise build only to discard: managed daily backups replace a nightly-export scheduler and storage integration, and the native exception-reporting integration replaces a bespoke error pipeline. We keep the drills; we stop maintaining plumbing before revenue.
6. **Reply detection gate (Step 7), carried: attempt only with a zero-marginal-cost IMAP mailbox; recommend skip.** Roadmap Step 10 stays pending the Step 9 fallback either way.

### Step 2: Change-speed rails: releases, previews, and eyes

**Outcome:** Priority 1. Daily shipping that survives paying teams: releases follow an expand-migrate discipline whose rollback target is always schema-compatible, every PR gets a working isolated signed-in preview, and production failures alert a named operator, not a log nobody reads.

**Build:**
- **Release discipline, launch scope:** every release touching persisted data is classed **expand** (additive schema: new fields optional; old documents remain valid) or **migrate** (backfill via the first-party `@convex-dev/migrations` component: online, batched, resumable). **Destructive contraction is deferred at launch (see exclusions): removed-from-use fields stay in the schema, marked `retired` in metadata, invisible in the UI.** The rollback target of a migrate release is the expand release that introduced its fields; that target's schema accepts backfilled documents by construction, and nothing ever deletes a field the target requires. CI requires every release PR to declare its class and rollback-target release in a structured release-notes field and blocks merge without it; no bespoke schema-diff tool.
- **CI gate and serialization:** GitHub Actions on every PR and main: typecheck, backend tests, MCP build and tests, web build, release-notes check, pinned `/api/v1` contract test. **Releases classed expand or migrate additionally validate their schema against a restored backup snapshot before deploy; releases that touch no persisted data (copy, styling, pure UI) skip the snapshot rehearsal.** Prod deploys only from green main, serialized, deploying the exact tested commit, exposed at `/version`.
- **Previews, reproducible:** one CI workflow per PR provisions a Convex preview deployment, configures preview authentication via AuthKit auto-provisioning (docs.convex.dev/auth/authkit/auto-provision; driver verifies), seeds fixtures through a new **internal** seed function (the current `pnpm seed` needs an orgId and an authenticated admin, so it cannot serve), and publishes a Worker preview URL bound to exactly that backend. Teardown on PR close.
- **Observability, detection not storage:** Convex Pro's native exception-reporting integration (docs.convex.dev/production/integrations/exception-reporting; driver verifies setup) captures function exceptions with release context, **and its alert rule and destination are configured as part of this build so that a new production exception notifies Shakur (operator of record) directly**. What stays ours is application-specific: a small `opsEvents` table plus health crons for stalled migrations, sync failures (Step 6), and billing reconciliation drift (Step 3), each firing a webhook notification to Shakur. The agent REST 500 handler **forwards the underlying exception to the reporting service** (not merely a local table) before returning its generic body. `docs/OPERATIONS.md` is the runbook: what each alert means, the rollback command, the restore procedure, the Step 3 exit procedures. No customer contents or credentials in any report.
- **Backups:** Convex Pro managed daily backups; **the published retention policy states the actual configured managed retention (Convex documents seven days for daily backups; driver verifies the configured value)**; the restore drill below stays. No bespoke export scheduler exists.
- **Rollback:** `rollback <sha>` CI job redeploys a named release, constrained to declared valid targets; the Worker rolls back by version routing.
- **Flags:** an org-settings `flags` map read by UI and functions, **writable only by internal operator functions**, so a customer cannot enable an unreleased feature.
- **Rate limiting:** first-party rate limiter component on agent REST writes, per key, generous, clear 429s.

**Prove it:** Start: Step 1 live; a seeded second test org; an old browser session and a released MCP client left running. Actions and expectations: (1) a PR with a failing test blocks deploy; its preview still builds. (2) A release PR touching persisted data without class and rollback-target declared: blocked; declared, it merges and its snapshot validation runs; a pure-UI PR skips the snapshot rehearsal and still runs full tests. (3) **Migration and rollback drill against the real target:** ship the expand release (optional field, tolerant code); start the backfill over 1,000+ records on prod while the old browser session and old MCP client keep reading and writing; no client errors; mid-backfill, ship a migrate-class behavior change; then `rollback` **to the expand release**: the deploy succeeds (its schema retains the field, so backfilled documents validate), the writes made during the backfill are still present and readable, both old clients still work, and `/version` confirms the target within 10 minutes end to end; an attempted rollback past the expand release is refused by the job. (4) Two concurrent PR previews: IV signs in on both, edits data in each; no cross-talk with each other or prod; each preview's `/version` matches its PR head. (5) **Detection drill:** inject a thrown exception in a staging copy of a background job and a request through the caught REST-500 path, plus a stalled-migration condition: the uncaught exception and the forwarded REST exception **both appear in the reporting service and both generate their own operator notification to Shakur**; the stall fires the health cron's notification; none contains record contents; the runbook resolves all three. (6) Restore drill: a Pro backup restored to a scratch deployment matches the source by the canonical comparison script. (7) Operator enables a flag for the test org: visible there within 2 seconds, absent elsewhere; a client attempt to write flags is rejected with no change. (8) An agent write burst gets 429 with no partial writes. Pass: all eight. Fail: any deploy past a red gate, any old-client error or lost write across the migrate-and-rollback cycle, any rollback that fails schema validation, any preview cross-talk, or **any injected failure that reaches storage but not Shakur**. Recovery: the drills are the rehearsals; failing one blocks declaring Step 2 done.

**Evidence and verifier:** CI links, the blocked and passing release PRs, snapshot-validation output, the migration-plus-rollback transcript with old-client logs, both preview URLs and isolation checks, the reporting-service alert screenshots and received notifications beside `opsEvents`, restore comparison, flag rejection response. IV re-runs drills 3, 4, and 5 from the plan text. **PENDING**.

### Step 3: Charging: every state, boundary, race, and exit is defined

**Outcome:** Priority 2. A team trials without a card, pays a flat workspace price, recovers from failed payments without gaining a second subscription or immortal grace, returns after a terminal cancellation, cancels with the published behavior, exports everything, and can be deleted (workspace and person alike); entitlement is enforced on every write path; external charging opens only behind a commercial gate with a verified tax decision.

**Build:**
- **Component first:** evaluate `@convex-dev/stripe` (capabilities **unverified**; the components page fetch failed 2026-09-24); adopt where it genuinely replaces plumbing. Entitlement rules stay Remold's own code; every test below applies regardless.
- **Entitlement model:** `billing` keyed by orgId: `entitlement` (`trial`, `active`, `grace`, `readonly`), `stripeCustomerId?`, `subscriptionId?`, `stripeStatus?`, `trialEndsAt`, `graceEndsAt?`, `delinquencyEpisodeId?`. Transitions: org creation starts `trial`, `trialEndsAt` = now + 14 days on an injectable clock. Trial past deadline, no subscription: `readonly`. Checkout completed: `active`. **Delinquency: on first observing `past_due`, open an episode and set `graceEndsAt` once; repeated reconciliation within the same episode never moves it; the episode closes only on payment success.** Grace expired, or Stripe `unpaid`: `readonly`. **Cancellation, two distinct things: `cancel_at_period_end` on an active subscription is a pending cancellation, `active` until period end then `readonly`; terminal `status: canceled` (immediate cancel, or cancellation after failed retries) is `readonly` now.** `incomplete` and `incomplete_expired`: no subscription exists for entitlement purposes; **entitlement derives from the local trial clock (trial if `trialEndsAt` is future, else readonly), never from prior entitlement.** **Recovery, split by whether the subscription is recoverable: while it is `past_due` or `unpaid`, recovery uses the existing subscription's portal payment-recovery flow and new Checkout is refused; once the subscription is terminally `canceled`, it no longer counts as live, so the one-live-subscription check passes and a new reserved Checkout attempt is offered; on its completion the old delinquency episode is closed as superseded and controls nothing.** `readonly` never deletes anything.
- **Purchase race:** Checkout is guarded by an **atomic per-org purchase reservation** (a unique reservation row inserted in a mutation; a second concurrent caller finds it and is handed the same pending session). The Stripe Checkout session is created with a **stable idempotency key derived from the reservation id**, so a crash after session creation retries into the same session. The reservation is released only when reconciliation confirms completion (subscription live) or session expiry. Combined with the server-side one-live-subscription check, an org cannot end up with two subscriptions.
- **Stripe processing, reconciliation not replay:** webhook events (signature over raw body) are recorded and serve only as triggers; the serialized per-org handler fetches the subscription's current authoritative state from Stripe and derives entitlement, so stale or duplicate events cannot regress state; each processed event and its effect commit atomically. A daily reconciliation cron sweeps every org with a `stripeCustomerId`, corrects drift, and logs it to `opsEvents`.
- **Enforcement at the choke points:** `requireWritable(orgId)` inside `applyChange` (UI, CSV import, suggestion apply, agent direct writes, automation writes in one place) and in the explicit list of non-record mutations (object and field editing, invites, integrations). **Always permitted:** member removal, role changes, agent key revocation, billing actions, export. Agent writes on a readonly org get a clear 402-style refusal.
- **Boundaries:** Checkout sessions and portal links are created server-side from the caller's membership using the org's own `stripeCustomerId` and the fixed price id; client-supplied customer, price, or org identifiers are never honored; billing requires admin or owner.
- **Exit, workspace:** owner-scoped **full export**: JSON archive of object and field definitions, records with stable ids and three-word refs, links, and event history (CSV stays for spreadsheets, not as the exit vehicle). Workspace deletion is an authenticated operator-run procedure: verify the requesting owner, cancel any subscription, delete the org's rows across all tables, revoke its keys, log to `opsEvents`; **the published data-retention note states the configured managed-backup retention from Step 2**.
- **Exit, person:** an authenticated operator-run personal-account deletion in the runbook: verify the requester controls the account; transfer or confirm ownership where they are a sole owner; remove all memberships; revoke sessions; delete the WorkOS identity; and **anonymize the `users` row (name and email replaced, id retained) rather than delete it, documented as such, so other teams' records and event attribution stay intact**.
- **Commercial launch gate:** before any non-Shakur charge: published pricing page (price, currency, renewal, cancellation terms), refund policy, privacy page, support email; portal cancellation behavior matching the published promise; and a **verified tax decision recorded at gate time: the business's operating jurisdiction, whether this offering is taxable there including by physical presence, and any registration or collection setup that requires, decided with Shakur against primary sources then, not asserted in this plan**. Economic-nexus threshold monitoring is kept only for later expansion. Stripe's US tax guidance is the starting reference (docs.stripe.com/tax/supported-countries/united-states).

**Prove it:** Start: Step 2 rails; Stripe test mode; orgs A (owner, admin, member, agent key) and B, seeded; injectable clock for local time, Stripe test clocks for Stripe time. (1) A checks out: `active`, banner clears live, invoice in portal. (2) Renewal fails: `grace` opens an episode; **run the daily reconciliation through day 8: `graceEndsAt` never moves and readonly begins on schedule**; during readonly, a UI record write, CSV import, suggestion apply, agent write, and automation write are each refused with zero database writes; member removal, key revocation, export, billing still work; while the subscription is `past_due`, new Checkout is refused and portal recovery restores `active` within a minute of reconciliation, closes the episode, and Stripe shows exactly one subscription; a later new delinquency opens a fresh episode with a fresh deadline. (3) Cancellation trio: `cancel_at_period_end` keeps `active` until period end then `readonly`; an immediate cancel is `readonly` now; cancellation after exhausted retries (terminal `canceled`) is `readonly` now. **(3b) Return after terminal cancellation: from that canceled state, the org is offered a new reserved Checkout; completing it restores `active`, exactly one live subscription exists in Stripe, and the old delinquency episode no longer controls entitlement.** (4) `incomplete_expired` during a live local trial leaves `trial`; after `trialEndsAt`, it is `readonly`. (5) Purchase race: two concurrent Checkout calls for A yield one reservation and one session, and both callers proceeding through payment produce exactly one subscription; separately, a crash injected after session creation retries via the idempotency key into the same session. (6) Stale-event attack: a `past_due`-era event delivered after recovery leaves A `active`. (7) Outage: a simulated day without webhooks is corrected by the daily sweep, drift logged. (8) Trial expiry with no card: `readonly`, no Stripe objects. (9) Boundary battery, same database: B's owner forging A's identifiers into portal or Checkout calls is refused server-side; A's ordinary member is refused the portal by role; B stays fully writable while A is readonly; A's agent key reads nothing of B; a flag enabled for A is invisible to B. (10) Exit, workspace: A's owner runs the full export; the archive round-trips into a scratch org with definitions, records, refs, links, and history intact, including while A is readonly; operator deletion of A removes every A row and revokes A's keys while B is untouched by the canonical comparison. (11) Exit, person: a user belonging to A and B requests deletion; ownership is transferred where needed, memberships gone, sessions revoked, WorkOS identity deleted, the `users` row anonymized; B's records and event attribution still render, pointing at the anonymized profile; the deleted person can no longer sign in. (12) Forged and replayed webhooks: 401 and no-op. Live gate: commercial pages published, the tax decision recorded with its sources and Shakur's sign-off, then Shakur subscribes his own org with a real card, verifies the live invoice, cancels through the portal observing the published behavior, and refunds. Pass: all thirteen plus the gate. Fail: any write during readonly, any client-writable entitlement, any boundary crossing, any second live subscription, any moving grace deadline, **any canceled customer with no path back**, any exit gap. Recovery: entitlement is derived state; drift is repaired by reconciliation against Stripe, which is the tested path.

**Evidence and verifier:** test-clock and injectable-clock logs beside `billing` history, day-8 sweep output, the cancellation trio's states plus the 3b repurchase transcript, the single-subscription evidence from Stripe's dashboard, refusal responses per write path, export archive and round-trip diff, deletion comparisons for workspace and person, the live invoice and refund, the recorded tax decision. IV runs cases 2, 3, 3b, 5, 7, 9, and 11 from the plan text. Shakur: Stripe account, the price, the tax decision, the live-mode test. **PENDING**.

### Step 6: The Remold-Mautic sync, r3 design with the create/delete race closed

**Outcome:** People in the Codemyvibe org appear in Mautic within minutes; unsubscribes and form submissions come back onto records; the sync survives crashes, partial batches, missed webhooks, out-of-order delivery, and interleaved create-and-delete, without duplicates, echoes, orphans, or stale consent.

**Build:** The r3 M5 design governs; summary plus the fix. One integration per org, one org per Mautic base URL, configured by an internal operator function for Codemyvibe only; no customer settings surface. `mauticLinks` carry the record's three-word `ref` and state `pendingCreate | linked | pendingDelete | remoteDeleted`; links are written before remote creation so recovery always has an identity. Race fix: a deletion request is durable, never destructive on arrival; deleting a record whose link is `pendingCreate` sets `pendingDelete` and stops; the serialized per-org sync worker finalizes a `pendingDelete` only after the outstanding creation is reconciled (the `remold_ref` search finds the contact, which is then deleted, or the create is confirmed never committed), and only then removes the link. Strict one-way field ownership (identity fields pushed via PATCH; consent, points, activity pulled back through `applyChange` as attributed automation events) structurally prevents loops. Per-item batch handling, durable cursors, three-strikes visible disable. `created`-origin deletes remove the remote contact, `adopted` only unlink; remote deletion parks the link `remoteDeleted` with explicit operator resync as the only recreate path. A segment pass diffs `Campaign.people` links. Webhooks HMAC-verified over the raw body; submission effects and dedup markers commit in one mutation; the polling checkpoint advances only after a completed traversal, markers being the dedup authority; consent reconciles current state, never replayed events. Step 2 flags keep everything dark outside Codemyvibe.

**Prove it:** The r3 battery plus the race case: first-sync with adoption and duplicate-email skip; PATCH isolation; unsubscribe round-trip inside a minute; idle-run zero-diff; crash after remote create with `remold_ref` recovery; crash then record deletion before retry (orphan cleanup); the delayed-create race (hold the outbound create with a test flag, delete the record, release the create: no remote contact and no link remains, in either completion order); injected batch item failure; both delete directions plus no-silent-recreation over ten runs and one explicit resync; webhook outage with partial delivery and consent flips ending correct; segment add and remove of an unedited Person; forged webhook 401 with a valid batched control; a second org's connection attempt refused. Pass: all. Fail: any duplicate, echo, orphan, unfinished sync state, lost or doubled submission, stale consent, or cross-org touch.

**Evidence and verifier:** per-case transcripts, Mautic contact export beside Remold events export, webhook logs. IV independently reruns the delayed-create race, the delete directions, and the outage case. **PENDING**.

A stranger finds remoldcrm.com, signs up through AuthKit, names her org, and lands on "Add your first client"; her first record exists inside five minutes (Steps 1, 4). She sends her cofounder the invite link; he lands in her org after hosted sign-in and watches her edit appear live. She pastes one npx command into Claude Code; its first proposal waits in the queue until she taps apply (Step 4). On day 14 the trial banner shows the published price and terms; she checks out and the banner clears before she is back on Today (Step 3). Months later her card fails; she works through the grace week whose deadline never moves, the org then goes read-only with export one click away, and the portal's recovery flow restores her in a minute without a second subscription; had the subscription lapsed all the way to cancellation, a fresh checkout would have brought her back just the same (Step 3). The Remold release that shipped that morning declared its rollback target, backfilled online under her live session, and could have rolled back to its expand release without losing her writes (Step 2). She found Remold through a campaign CodeMyVibe's agent proposed, Shakur applied, and Mautic sent from mail.codemyvibe.com, whose unsubscribes flowed back onto the right records (Steps 5-7).

- **One org per Mautic is a v1 simplification**; a customer-facing connector needs ownership isolation designed and reviewed first.
- **Pricing (the number) is unset**; Step 3 is structurally complete without it, but the live gate waits on Shakur's choice.
