Shared contract that every job must use

C1. Ownership and tenant identity

Convex owns workspace membership, customer records, agent identity/authority, desired campaigns/posts, approval versions, commercial documents, work assignments and the customer-visible audit. Engines own execution details and observed provider outcomes. The payment provider owns settlement truth. Paperclip, if selected, mirrors assignments and owns execution attempts; it must not become a second editable CRM/task system. CRM writes use applyChange; command/lifecycle/financial state uses narrowly validated domain mutations plus attributed events. Do not force control-plane tables through generic record CRUD.

A binding key is (provider, environment, providerAccountOrInstance, resourceKind, externalId), mapped to exactly one org and local resource. IDs alone are not globally unique: two Mautic instances may both have contact 1. Resolve bindings server-side; never trust org, actor, price, connected-account ID or credentials from a client/callback. Create durable provisioning intent before an external create; retry resolves the same binding. Opaque IDs and unchanged B-tenant comparisons must be tested in the same deployed service topology.

C2. Authority and the one approval queue

Humans and agents use the same domain action functions. Every agent has origin hosted/external, state active/suspended/fired, monotonically increasing authorityEpoch, and capabilities scoped to objects/records, fields, campaign, social account, merchant account and money/volume limits. Categories are read, draft/propose, record mutation, marketing.send, social.publish, billing.issue/collect/refund, agent.manage and delegation. Default is read only for explicitly selected domains plus propose; hire-time choices can grant direct action. Owner-controlled settings cannot be self-escalated by agents. Delegation is the intersection of the grantor's scope and a recorded delegation ceiling; no manager can grant broader rights or increase its own budget. Owner control remains human. Agent.manage is restricted to an explicitly granted delegation subtree, never the actor itself, its ancestors, unrelated peers or human memberships. Authority delegation records its parent grant and epoch chain; reporting lines alone grant nothing. Every current check validates the complete bounded, acyclic grant chain. Firing/downgrading a grantor immediately invalidates grants derived from that authority, including descendant queued/running claims; descendants retain only independently valid human grants. Materializing descendant epochs can be asynchronous because ancestor validation already refuses stale authority. Only an authorized human or still-valid in-scope manager may regrant within its own ceiling.

Approval binds the content hash, audience snapshot/version, destination account, schedule, amount/currency, workflow version and expiry. Any material edit invalidates approval. Dynamic audience changes require a new approval unless the grant explicitly authorizes the bounded dynamic rule; consent is always rechecked. Approval does not change artifact ownership. Reassignment explicitly transfers accountable ownership and requires current authority. Existing suggestions from fired agents remain visible but paused: a human may adopt one through the same approval queue as a new action with the human as actor and accountable owner under their current authority, preserving original authorship. Old approval cannot revive the fired agent.

Every read path applies field/record masks, including search, history, suggestions, reports, exports, MCP, REST, files, provider outputs and live subscriptions. A marketing agent does not gain invoice access by being in the same org. Separate service credentials are held by trusted adapters/vault, never handed to agents. Hosted model credentials are available only inside the trusted model adapter. Secret references, rotation, backups and logs must not expose values.

C3. External action and cancellation contract

One durable operation per logical action: org, server-derived actor, accountable owner, capability, authorityEpoch, approved immutable payload version, binding, idempotency key, reservation, lease/fencing version, attempts, result and provider references. States: proposed, approved, queued, dispatching, confirmed, refused, paused, cancellationPending, cancelled, outcomeUnknown. A recoverable failure carries next action; terminal failure does not trigger a fresh logical send. Transactionally claim and reserve; at a final atomic dispatch-permit mutation revalidate epoch/state, scope, artifact/approval, entitlement, account health, budget and applicable consent. This mutation is the local linearization point: revocation committed before it refuses the action; revocation after it leaves one bounded, already-permitted attempt in-flight even if provider acceptance occurs later. A permit is one-use, short-lived and bound to exact operation/version/account/worker fencing version; retry requires a new current-authority check. Re-check every continuation, not only the first job call. Expired workers cannot commit using an old fencing version. Explicit limits on outstanding permits, recipients per batch and per-call exposure bound the in-flight tail; no unbounded batch can hide behind one permit.

Timeout after a provider may have accepted means outcomeUnknown. Reconcile by the same provider account/idempotency key/external reference before retry. Never blindly resend an uncertain email/post/charge or restore a queue by replaying all events. Bound retry/backoff; unsupported idempotency requires a provider lookup or operator resolution, not a fabricated exactly-once promise.

Firing commits the new epoch and stops new claims immediately. It cancels queued work, asks running runtimes to stop, and places owned unexecuted posts/campaigns/suggestions and their descendant work on pause. Running dispatches are reconciled and visible; an accepted email or charge cannot be recalled by our transaction. UI distinguishes “access revoked”, “cancellation pending” and “stopped”; never show stopped before confirmations. A late confirmed outcome is recorded as completed after revocation, not hidden. Authority reduction follows the same rules for newly forbidden work. A hung runtime loses tool access even before process termination succeeds. The trusted model adapter checks the run and current grant-chain epoch for every new model call and continuation. It requests cancellation of an active stream on revocation; an already-permitted call may still incur bounded residual cost, which remains reserved and is reconciled visibly. Do not promise cancellation erases already-started provider compute.

Prefer Remold-held social schedules and send just in time. If an engine holds schedules (especially Mautic campaigns), the proof must demonstrate a transport/plugin hook through the current-authority dispatch-permit boundary for each send; removing a campaign later is insufficient. Test a hold before the final permit mutation, fire, release, and require refusal; separately hold after the permit but before provider response, fire and require honest in-flight/late-outcome reconciliation. Also test response loss after actual acceptance. No new permits after revocation is the guarantee, not impossibly atomic control over a remote provider. If interception requires a maintained fork of Mautic core rather than a supported plugin/transport hook, reject that Mautic candidate for this pack and compare the native alternative; any proposed core fork requires a newly reviewed selection record. Do not silently weaken firing semantics.

C4. Callbacks, consent and budgets

Callbacks authenticate raw payloads using the provider-supported mechanism (signature/mTLS or an independently authenticated pull if unavailable), scope to a trusted endpoint/account/environment binding, and durably deduplicate (binding,eventId) with the state effect. They cannot mint authority. Re-fetch mutable provider state for out-of-order notifications; append-only form submissions and run results need their own stable dedup identity and payload integrity instead. A valid signed callback still cannot substitute another tenant's resource. Polling resumes only after a fully traversed checkpoint; tolerate partial batches and missed webhooks.

Consent is per recipient, channel and purpose, with source/time/version and suppression precedence. People may record valid consent changes through a dedicated workflow; generic record edits cannot forge provider payment or delivery truth. Unsubscribe, complaint and bounce handling pauses delivery independently of the CRM sync delay. Transactional invoices/receipts are separate from marketing. Public forms/landing pages use tenant-bound routes, validation, rate limits and duplicate-safe submissions; preview activity is not counted as real traffic. Re-import cannot erase suppression. Retention/deletion basis for minimal suppression data is settled at the commercial gate, not assumed indefinitely lawful.

Hosted runs reserve bounded maximum exposure before starting; settle authoritative usage once. Enforce per-run duration/steps/tokens/tool calls, per-org and global concurrency and spend ceilings. Defaults: zero live paid usage until an approved account and numeric cap exist. If provider limits cannot bound exposure, that runtime cannot advertise a hard cap or pass the hosted proof. Missing usage keeps the reservation and pauses new spend; BYO keys still need hard limits. Never reuse Shakur’s subscription or host-home connectors for customer execution. Hosted and external agent controls share the same capabilities; we can revoke external agents' Remold access, not control unrelated tools on their machines.

C5. Two money systems and exit

Platform subscription billing uses Remold's Stripe account. Client invoices/payments use a workspace-bound connected merchant account and separate identifiers, webhook ingress and reconciliation. Proposed proof route: direct charges with each workspace as merchant, hosted/embedded onboarding and Remold controls for ordinary work. Exact account configuration, liability, supported countries and taxes require current provider evidence before live use; never assert customer tax obligations disappear from platform responsibilities. No application fee is introduced by default.

Money uses integer minor units plus currency, never generic float totals. Quotes and issued invoice snapshots are versioned; acceptance references one immutable version. Issued document numbers/tax details follow the approved jurisdiction policy. Deleted CRM people/companies cannot rewrite issued financial documents. Corrections use void/credit/refund workflows. Public acceptance/payment links are unguessable, scoped, expiring/revocable as applicable, rate-limited, and bound to the exact document/account; webhook truth, not the browser success redirect, marks paid.

Proposed operational default, explicitly a G-pay confirmation gate: a lapsed Remold subscription stops new drafts/issuance/outbound marketing/social/hosted runs; already-authorized invoice collections and recurring client payments continue, with incoming financial/consent reconciliation, refunds/cancellation, security cleanup and exports available. Those allowlisted system/safety writes bypass readonly; general writes do not. Cancellation of a client recurring service is distinct from cancellation of Remold. No live commercial behavior until this published rule is confirmed.

Export covers customer records, definitions, links/history, campaigns/assets/consent/suppression, posts, agents/tasks/run artifacts/operations and financial documents/statuses; never secrets. Workspace deletion disables dispatch first, drains/reconciles in-flight effects, cancels customer commitments according to explicit instructions, exports, then deletes/deprovisions engines and personal data under the retention policy. Financial retention and account ownership rules may retain named data with a stated reason/expiry; do not promise indiscriminate destruction. Person deletion removes engine copies, memberships and sessions, with minimal lawful attribution retained. Partial deletion is resumable and visible. Restore all required databases/files/keys to scratch, reconcile uncertain operations without resend or double-charge, and reapply post-backup revocations/suppressions before enabling egress.
