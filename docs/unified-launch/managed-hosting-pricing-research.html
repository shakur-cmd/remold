Your calls

  • License. AGPL can't do what you want. It explicitly allows anyone to sell the software. To allow self-hosting but block resale you need a different license. I recommend FSL-1.1-ALv2 (the license Convex uses). Anyone can self-host it for their own business, but nobody can sell it as a competing service. Each version becomes Apache-2.0 after two years. The cost: Remold would be "source-available", not "open source". That reverses the AGPL-3.0-only decision from 2026-09-22 and the project rules. Switching is clean now, because you're the only committer and there are 0 forks. Copies already published stay AGPL for anyone who took them.
  • Managed price. Proposed to test: $299/month per business. It covers hosting, unlimited users, and a fixed monthly allowance of changes you make for them. Texting, calling, email sending and AI are passed through at cost with a cap they set. The $49 self-serve price from this morning stays separate. This is a price to test, not a proven one.
  • Change allowance. Pick the number, e.g. 2 small changes a month. Until you set one, it's zero. "Unlimited changes" is the one thing I'd never offer at a flat price.

What this means for Remold

  • Unlimited users is table stakes. All four do it. It's not a differentiator, it's the entry ticket.
  • Nobody at these prices changes the software for you. GoHighLevel's answer is agencies reselling it, and GoHighLevel's own blog suggests they charge clients "$97 – $497+". That's the closest match to your offer. Your edge is the human doing the changes, not the feature list.
  • Be honest about usage up front. Surprise charges and wallet top-ups are GoHighLevel's loudest complaint. Say in one line that texts, calls, email and AI are billed at cost, capped at a limit they set. That beats a fake "all included".
  • Why $299 and not $99. PipeBase at $99 is cheaper software with bundled calling. But your plan includes your time, and your time is the scarce part. $299 matches GoHighLevel Unlimited and Conduyt Starter, and neither of them makes changes for you. If $299 won't sell in your in-person conversations, lower the change allowance before you lower the price.
  • Conduyt is the closest product. Flat price, AI plus MCP, self-hosting on Enterprise. It's also unproven, so don't treat its price as market evidence.

The license question, simply

LicenseSelf-host for own businessSell it as a serviceCalled open source?
AGPL-3.0 (today)YesYes. §4: "You may charge any price or no price". §10 forbids adding a no-selling rule. They must publish their changes (§13).Yes
FSL-1.1-ALv2 (recommended)Yes: "internal use" and professional services are permittedNo, while the version is under 2 years old. Blocks any "commercial product or service that substitutes for the Software".No, "source-available"
PolyForm NoncommercialNo for businesses. Only personal, charity, education and government use.NoNo

PolyForm matches your words literally, but it would stop a small business from self-hosting for itself. FSL matches what you actually described. Either way, Remold stops qualifying as open source, because the Open Source Definition #6 forbids restricting business use. That's a positioning change worth a real decision, not a footnote. If you keep AGPL, the realistic protection is that competitors must publish their changes, and customers buy you, not the code.
